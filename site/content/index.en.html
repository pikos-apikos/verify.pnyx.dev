The verification path

One claim. A visible chain of evidence.

The mechanism is the same across countries and languages. The authorities and sources change; the evidentiary contract does not.

  1. 01

    Define the claim

    Separate fact from opinion. Resolve entities, dates, quantities, jurisdiction, product version, and the exact proposition that can be tested.

  2. 02

    Route to the right sources

    Search primary authorities, official records, original research, manufacturer documentation, and credible independent evidence across languages.

  3. 03

    Preserve the evidence

    Store the relevant excerpt, locator, timestamp, version, URL, and content hash—not merely a link or a generated summary.

  4. 04

    Test support and conflict

    Compare definitions, time periods, source independence, and whether each item supports, contradicts, contextualises, or fails to address the claim.

  5. 05

    Expose uncertainty

    Report status, evidence quality, coverage, agreement, temporal fit, model uncertainty, and review state as separate signals.

  6. 06

    Escalate judgment

    High-impact claims require qualified human review. When the evidence is insufficient, the correct result is to abstain.

What must remain true

Verification that can travel.

01

Global by design

No country is the system boundary. Claims may require evidence from any jurisdiction.

02

Multilingual by default

A claim in one language may be verified by primary evidence published in another.

03

Evidence first

The model may help search and explain. It may not invent the evidentiary record.

04

Uncertainty stays visible

Conflicts, gaps, revisions, and failed searches remain part of the result.

05

Humans keep judgment

Sensitive conclusions remain subject to accountable human review.

06

Provenance becomes memory

Versioned verification records let people and machines revisit what changed and why.

Project status

The contract comes first.

Verify is at the foundation stage. This site states the public contract and research direction; it does not present a public verification service as already operational.

The first implementation target is a small, inspectable verification flow for claims that can be checked with strong primary evidence—such as product specifications and well-established scientific facts.
